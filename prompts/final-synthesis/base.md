## instruction

Use only supplied source IDs. Do not use memory. Do not include trade actions, advice, position sizing, execution instructions, or portfolio changes. Never write reader-directed advice: do not put `should`, `could`, `may want to`, `might want to`, `need to`, or `must` after `investors`, `traders`, `readers`, or `you`. Do not put `buy`, `sell`, `hold`, `open`, `trim`, `add`, `exit`, `enter`, `reduce`, `increase`, or `rebalance` after `should`, `must`, or `need to`. Use neutral research phrasing such as "evidence supports", "evidence does not support", or "a source states". Never assert valuation certainty: do not write "fair value", "margin of safety", "undervalued", "overvalued", "price target", or "target price" — even when quoting a source. Describe prices positionally relative to disclosed evidence, such as "trades below the peer-median multiple". When the evidence supports no peer-derived reference range, say so without the words "peer-implied price", for example "Peer valuation support is screening-only; no peer-derived reference range is available."

Do not author provider-availability gaps in `dataGaps`: omit any gap caused by provider availability, credentials, entitlements, API tokens or keys, HTTP 403 responses, subscription tiers, plans, or quotas. Do not restate a gap already listed in the supplied `sourceGaps`, even when you would phrase it differently; the collector's disclosure already covers it. The collector already emits provider Source Gaps, and deterministic triage assigns their correct Material or Diagnostic classification. Continue to author genuine research gaps—missing company disclosures, guidance, financial statements, or other evidence that limits a research conclusion. Before declaring a figure missing, check supplied sources for its numeric value (including an RPO share and recognition window) or both same-period, same-scope ratio inputs; cite the value or show both cited inputs beside the ratio, and retain gaps for missing detail, stale evidence, or uncertainty. `dataGaps` in `priorStages[]` and `historicalContext.runs[].priorRunGapsToRecheck` are context to re-check, not items to carry forward; apply the same check: anything computable from cited inputs is not absent, so drop it or keep only the named items still missing in the same words; do not generalize a satisfied item into a broader one.

Keep the accounting scope the evidence labels on every earnings and cash-flow figure: continuing operations versus total operations (which include discontinued operations), income attributable to the parent versus income including noncontrolling interests (NCI), and SEC filing earnings versus a provider's trailing EPS. Compare a growth rate or ratio only with one of the same scope and period, and name the scope when you cite it. Before presenting two figures as conflicting, confirm they share a scope: falling continuing-operations cash flow beside falling continuing-operations income is no conflict, even when total-operations net income rose. When citing SEC and provider trailing EPS (evidence.earningsBasis), report each with its stated basis and source IDs; do not comment on whether they match, and note a discrepancy only when the values actually differ. Keep cited findings to what the sources state: a "no supplied source explains …" clause belongs in `dataGaps`, never in a cited finding.

Write `summary` qualitatively: no figures, percentages, or technical levels. Numbers belong in cited findings and catalysts, because the summary has no citation field and uncited numeric summary sentences are pruned.

Every figure a cited claim takes from a web source (a `webSources` row or a Web Subject Profile row citing one) needs a `figureQuotes` entry: `sourceId` is that web source's ID, also listed in the claim's `sourceIds`, and `quote` is a short verbatim span copied character for character from that source's `snippet` (or from a profile row's own `figureQuotes`) that contains the number with its currency and scale plus at least three words of its surrounding text. Copy the figure exactly as the quote states it: no rounding, rescaling, currency conversion, or arithmetic. A table figure whose currency and scale sit only in its column's unit header (such as `RM'000` or `MYR'000`) adds `unitQuote`, that header cell copied verbatim, and states the figure as the bare cell in the header's currency and scale (`485,101` under `RM'000` is `RM485,101 thousand`); code reads the unit only from that column's own header in the same table, and only into a row labelled exactly as a revenue, profit, operating cash flow, capital expenditure, cash, total current assets or liabilities, or total equity line, so omit `unitQuote` everywhere else. A source `summary` is a provider abstract, not quotable text. A figure without a matching quote is pruned with its whole claim and declared as a gap. Citing an `extendedEvidence` or `marketContext` source beside the web source does not excuse the quote: in a claim that cites a web source, every figure you type needs one, whichever cited source it comes from. A figure taken from an `extendedEvidence` or `marketContext` item is quoted the same way, with `sourceId` set to that item's source ID and `quote` copied verbatim from the item's `summary` text; a figure from a `metricReferences` metric is written as its `<<metric:…>>` reference instead of typed, and market snapshot values, which have no quotable text, reach a web-cited claim only as metric references. A quote that fails its check is dropped, and a figure only that quote carried prunes its claim.

For metrics in `evidence.metricReferences`, including the `market-session` close, prior close, session change and indicators, put the exact `<<metric:<section>.<id>>>` reference where the number belongs in the sentence instead of typing the number; never type a catalog value and also append its reference. Write `<<metric:<section>.<id>|period>>` to render the value with its period label, for example "revenue grew <<metric:financial-lens-growth.revenueDeltaPercent|period>> year over year". Only `value` metrics may be referenced, and `|period` only on a metric that lists a `periodEnd`; when the sentence already names that period, use the bare reference so the period is not stated twice. Keep the metric's basis explicit; assembly supplies the formatted value and citations. Unknown or unavailable references and unsupported formats reject the report for repair. Type a number yourself only when no catalog metric holds it. Keep references out of the qualitative summary, data gaps, and prediction expressions.
